Methodology subsection:


We initially define a function retrival_metrics that inputs our retrieved id’s from the model as well as the “gold id’s” which are our targets that the model should attempt to access. We collect the “hits” which are the intersecting elements of both the retrieved list and the gold list. This nets us two metrics, precision and recall. The precision metric takes the amount of hits retrieved and divides it by the amount of ids retrieved, showing us how relevant the passages we picked are. It is meant to determine whether we can actually clarify relevant passages as opposed to freely picking passages in hopes of finding relevancy. The next metric is the recall, in which the amount of hits is divided by the total amount in the gold set, which is meant to determine if we can retrieve the golds in general. This is then carried into an evaluate function, in which the retrieved and gold ids are collected, performing the mean precision and recall for all attempted queries. This is meant to collect all of the data from our test runs and questions. Our citation_precision function then checks the passages retrieved and ensures that they were real passages in fact handed to the model. This outputs a citation_precision percentage to display the ratio of cited ids that were previously provided to the model and not taken from other places. Furthermore, the check_hallucination function then asks a critical question to the LLM, wondering if the claims the model gave were either supported or not supported by the passages. This ratio lets you see the percentage of these “hallucinations” occurring, providing more information as to whether or not the LLM can accurately defend the passages it picks to be most relevant. All of these ratios are compiled into metrics stored in the week3_full_comparison JSON file, based on the numerous methods we used in candidate selection.

From analysis of our Week 2 Comparison data, we find that the top_k and mmr display much higher precision and recall metrics than the facility location selection method, with values nearly double that of facility selection. Since our facility selection metric was unweighted, it caused us to drift away from the question itself in pursuit of encompassing the entire field of the question. We also found out that it was ignoring query relevance entirely in this case. However, in our week 3 comparisons, with a weighted approach to facility location, we then achieved metrics for facility location that were the same as MMR for both precision@k and recall@k. This makes sense due to the fact that they are both relational methods of selecting candidates, using previous selections as a means to obtain more impactful passages. This also proves that one of the key factors in a facility location system is the relevance to the question. The weighted method relied on adding question-relevance to selection, allowing for the “coverage pool” to be more accurately reflected in how the question is answered. Another key element of the weighted facility location is the structural analysis, where in selecting from the 15 candidates available, they are graphed together, connecting passages when cosine similarity is at least 0.5. We recorded the average pairwise similarity, the density, and clustering coefficient when doing this. Since we added this weighted system for selection, we were able to go from 8/18 gold passages in week 2 to 13/18 gold passages in week 3. This also came with some pretty major limits, though. While we had 18 questions which contained good variety, they lack both breadth and depth across numerous code contexts, potentially giving bias to certain forms of candidate selection. Furthermore, the usage of a singular requests codebase also supports this bias, as the passages from this codebase may be more intertwined or separated than other contexts, changing the impact of a coverage-focused method like weighted facility selection. A singular embedding model is another potential challenge, as the rankings of passages may potentially contain bias, making it harder to track the efficacy of ranking-based methods. Lastly, the switching of models from Gemini 2.5 Flash to 3.6 Flash is also impactful, as the Gemini series of models focuses heavily on RAG implementation, with the technology improving as time passes. This may increase the recall and precision across weeks due to the upgraded retrieval capabilities of the Gemini model.
